# 02. MIDI recording, piano roll, always-on capture

## Goal

Turn whatever is played on a MIDI keyboard or the Mac keyboard into clips that can be edited with the mouse. The metronome and count-in help players stay in time, overdub builds a part in layers, and always-on capture rescues a good improvisation that was played without recording.

At the end the user can: arm a track, press record in an empty slot, hear the count-in, play 4 bars, get a looping clip, open it with a double-click, fix notes and velocity, quantize, and save. Or just play freely, press "Capture" and get a clip from the last phrase.

## Scope

Included: metronome, count-in, MIDI recording into a Session slot, overdub, latency compensation for MIDI, quantize while recording and afterwards, piano roll, always-on MIDI capture with a "Capture" button, a per-track record source switch (MIDI / audio), recording of mod wheel, pitch bend and sustain movements.

Not included: recording into the Arrangement (03), audio recording and always-on audio capture (05), device parameter automation (06), MPE (phase 3).

---

## Task 2.1. Metronome and count-in

- The metronome is generated by the engine (`engine/Metronome.h`), not by a plugin. Click: a sine with a 30 ms exponential decay, 1500 Hz on the downbeat, 1000 Hz on the other beats, 6 dB louder on the downbeat. The sound is sample-accurate: the click starts exactly on the beat's sample inside the block.
- The metronome goes to the master after the master chain (so the master limiter from 06 does not compress it) and is not included in the export (04).
- Settings live in the right-click menu of the metronome button: volume (−∞…0 dB, default −6), "Only while recording", count-in: none / 1 / 2 / 4 bars (default 1), click sound: "Classic" / "Wood" (the second is a short noise burst through a 2 kHz band-pass filter).
- Count-in: when recording starts from a stopped transport, the position starts at `-countInBars * beatsPerBar`, the metronome plays, clips are not launched, and the position field shows a countdown ("−1. 3"). Recording actually begins at 1.1.1 (or at the chosen point).
- Model: `PROJECT metronome=0|1 metronomeVolumeDb metronomeOnlyRecording countInBars`.

## Task 2.2. MIDI recording into a slot

### Behavior

- An empty slot of an armed MIDI track shows a record circle. Clicking it:
  - if the transport is stopped, starts the transport (with a count-in if enabled), and recording starts at the next quantized launch boundary (`launchQuantise`, default 1 bar);
  - if the transport is playing, recording starts at the next quantized boundary.
- While recording, the slot is red with a pulsing circle, a length bar grows inside it and notes are drawn as they are played.
- Clicking the recording slot again (or launching another clip on this track, or the transport ● button) ends recording at the next quantized boundary. Clip length = a whole number of bars from the start to that boundary. The clip immediately starts playing in a loop.
- If "Fixed recording length" is on (● button menu: off / 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 bars), recording stops by itself after that many bars and the clip loops.
- `Cmd+Z` after recording removes the recorded clip entirely (one "Record clip" transaction).
- The ● button in the transport, when the Session view is open and nothing is selected, starts recording into the first empty slot of the selected scene on all armed tracks (session recording).

### How it works

- In the audio thread every recording track has a `RecordBuffer`: a preallocated array of 65,536 events `RecordedEvent { int64_t samplePos; uint8_t bytes[3]; uint8_t size; }`, where `samplePos` is the transport position in samples. The audio thread writes events into an SPSC queue (`AbstractFifo`), and the message thread drains it on a 30 Hz timer.
- The message thread assembles events into notes: a note-on opens a note, a note-off (or a note-on with velocity 0) closes the earliest open note of the same pitch. Notes still open at the end of recording are closed at the clip end.
- While recording, notes are kept in a temporary clip `RecordingClip` (not in the model), which the UI draws in the slot. When recording ends, the clip is written to the model in one transaction.
- Mod wheel (CC1), sustain (CC64), pitch bend and aftertouch are recorded into the clip as `CONTROLLER` children of the clip: `CC number value time` and `BEND value time` (new node types in the model). The engine plays them back together with the notes (`renderClip` in `core` is extended). Sustain on playback behaves as in live playing (Prisma already handles it).
- A **record source** switch on the track header: "MIDI" / "Audio". The `recordSource` value already exists in the model (01). In this stage "Audio" is disabled with the tooltip "Audio recording arrives in stage 5".

### MIDI latency

- The player hears sound `outputLatency` after the engine computed it, and MIDI reaches the engine up to one buffer late. So the recorded position of an event = block position + event offset within the block − `outputLatencySamples` − `blockSize / 2`.
- The default is calculated automatically; settings (a new "Recording" tab) have a manual adjustment of ±50 ms and a "Reset" button. Test: an event arriving in a block with position `P` and offset `o` is recorded at `P + o - autoOffset`.

## Task 2.3. Overdub and record quantize

- **OVR** button in the transport (overdub; here for Session): if it is on and record is pressed on a playing MIDI clip, new notes are added to the existing ones on every pass, old ones are not erased. Each overdub pass is one undo transaction, so it can be rolled back layer by layer.
- **Record quantize** (● button menu): off (default) / 1/4 / 1/8 / 1/8T / 1/16 / 1/16T / 1/32. The note start moves to the nearest grid line, the length is kept.
- Quantize algorithms live in `core/include/daw/engine/Quantize.h`: `quantizeNotes (notes, grid, strength, swing, quantizeEnds)` as pure functions with tests.

## Task 2.4. Piano roll

Opens in the detail panel with a double-click on a clip or `Shift+Tab` with a clip selected. `Shift+Tab` switches the panel between the clip editor and the device chain. The panel can be taller for the editor: double-clicking the divider expands it to 60% of the window height.

### Layout

```
┌ Clip ────────┬ ruler: bars, beats, loop length (bracket), start point ──────────┐
│ name, color  ├─────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ loop on      │keys     │ note grid                                                │
│ start / end  │ (play)  │                                                          │
│ grid ▾       │         │                                                          │
│ scale ▾ Fold │         │                                                          │
│ quantize     ├─────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│              │ Velocity│ velocity bars                                            │
└──────────────┴─────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- On the left, the clip properties panel (180 px wide): name, color, "Loop" on/off, loop start and end, start point (`startOffset`), grid, scale and root, Fold, a "Quantize…" button.
- Keys to the left of the grid: clicking plays the note on the track (through the engine, like the on-screen keyboard); selecting by key selects all notes of that pitch.
- Below the grid is a 64 px velocity lane. Below that is a controller lane with a selector: Modwheel, Pitch Bend, Sustain (collapsible).

### Grid and zoom

- Horizontal zoom: wheel with Cmd, pinch gesture, `Cmd+=`/`Cmd+-` keys (when the editor has focus). Grid step is "Adaptive" by default: lines no closer than 12 px, plus fixed 1/4…1/64 and triplets. `Cmd+1` / `Cmd+2` narrow / widen the grid, `Cmd+3` toggles triplets, `Cmd+4` toggles snap to grid.
- Vertical zoom: wheel with Alt, row height 6–24 px. On opening, the editor shows the range where the clip's notes are (or C3–C5 for an empty clip).
- Black keys are darker rows (`panel2`), bar lines are `line` at full brightness, beat lines at 60%, grid lines at 30%.
- Notes: rectangles in the clip color, opacity by velocity (40% at 1, 100% at 127), selected notes with a `text` border. The note name inside, if there is room.
- The clip playhead runs across the grid if the clip is playing.

### Editing

| Action | How |
|---|---|
| Draw a note | Double-click on empty space, or pencil mode (`B`) and click. Length = grid step, velocity = same as the last edited note (100 by default) |
| Delete | Double-click on a note, `Delete`, or the pencil on a note |
| Select | Click, Shift-click to add, rubber band on empty space, `Cmd+A` for all |
| Move | Drag a note; snaps to the grid, holding Cmd while dragging disables snapping. Arrows: ←/→ by a grid step, ↑/↓ by a semitone, Shift+↑/↓ by an octave |
| Change length | Drag the right edge of a note (the cursor changes within 6 px of the edge). Shift+←/→ changes the length of selected notes by a grid step |
| Copy | Alt+drag, `Cmd+C`/`Cmd+V` (paste at the cursor position), `Cmd+D` duplicates the selection right after it |
| Velocity | Drag a bar in the Velocity lane; Cmd+drag on a note up/down; a line drawn across several bars makes a ramp |
| Split | `Cmd+E` at the mouse cursor position |
| Join | `Cmd+J` merges selected adjacent notes of the same pitch |
| Preview | A new or dragged note sounds briefly (toggled with the headphones button in the panel) |

- The whole mouse gesture is one undo transaction. During the gesture the model changes immediately (the graph is rebuilt no more than 30 times per second, which is fine for the stage 01 engine).

### Scale and Fold

- Choice of root (C…B) and scale: major, minor, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Locrian, harmonic minor, melodic minor, major and minor pentatonic, blues, chromatic (default: off).
- Note rows outside the scale are dimmed. In "Scale only" mode (a toggle), new notes and pitch dragging land only on scale notes.
- **Fold**: only rows that contain notes are shown (or the scale rows, if a scale is selected and there are no notes).

### Quantize (Cmd+U)

- `Cmd+U` quantizes the selected notes (or all) with the current settings. `Cmd+Shift+U` opens a window: grid, strength 0–100%, swing 0–75%, "Quantize note ends", an "Apply" button. Settings are remembered in the project.
- Swing moves every second grid line forward by `swing * gridStep / 2` (classic drum machine swing).

### Clip loop

- The editor ruler has a loop bracket: drag its ends or the whole bracket. `startOffset` is a triangle on the ruler.
- If "Loop" is off, the clip in the Session view plays once from start to end and stops.
- `Cmd+L` on a note selection sets the loop around it.

## Task 2.5. Always-on MIDI capture (Capture)

### Buffer

- The engine constantly, regardless of recording, puts **all incoming** MIDI events from all enabled inputs and from the Mac keyboard into a ring buffer. Size: 200,000 events (enough for 10+ minutes of very dense playing). Event: `CaptureEvent { int64_t engineSample; double transportBeat; bool transportPlaying; int64_t trackIdForInput; uint8_t bytes[3]; }`, where `engineSample` is the sample counter since the engine started, and `trackIdForInput` is the track the event went to (the armed one), or 0.
- The audio thread writes into an SPSC queue, the message thread moves events into the `CaptureBuffer` ring buffer (in `core`, pure C++). Entries older than 10 minutes are evicted.

### "10 min" strip in the transport

- A narrow 130×24 px strip: time runs right to left, the last 10 minutes. Each note-on is a vertical tick in the track color, height by velocity. On the right, a red "now" line. Redraws at 15 fps, only if new events have arrived or time has passed.
- Tooltip: "Always-on capture: the last 10 minutes of playing. Press "Capture" or Cmd+Shift+C".

### Capture (Cmd+Shift+C or the "Capture" button)

The `capturePhrase` algorithm in `core/include/daw/engine/Capture.h`:
1. Take the events of the target track (the selected MIDI track; if it received no events, then all events with no track).
2. Find the last **phrase**: go backwards from the last event while the gap between adjacent note-ons is less than 4 seconds, or (if the transport was playing) less than 2 bars.
3. **The transport was playing during the phrase:** positions are taken from `transportBeat`. Clip start = the start of the bar of the first note. Length = a power of two in bars (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32), no shorter than the phrase. If the phrase repeated (the last N bars resemble the previous N bars by note set, similarity > 80%), the last full repetition is taken.
4. **The transport was stopped:** the tempo is estimated from the phrase (`estimateTempo`: a histogram of intervals between note-ons, candidates 60–200 BPM, picking the tempo with the most hits on a 1/16 grid with a 30 ms tolerance; if confidence is low, the current project tempo). If the project is empty (no clips), the project tempo is changed to the detected one; otherwise the phrase is stretched to the current tempo. The first downbeat is the first note.
5. The resulting clip goes into the first empty slot of the selected track starting from the selected scene (one "Capture MIDI" transaction), and, if the transport is playing, is launched with quantization. The hint line shows: "Captured 8 bars, 112 notes → Arp, scene Drop".
- Capture does not clear the buffer: the same phrase can be captured again into another track.

## Task 2.6. Mac keyboard and on-screen keyboard

- The Mac keyboard (`M`) works as a "Computer keyboard" MIDI input and is recorded on equal terms with a MIDI keyboard.
- The on-screen keyboard from phase 0 moves into a slide-out strip above the detail panel (`Cmd+Alt+K`), hidden by default.

## Task 2.7. Project scale, tuning and transposition

The owner's request after live testing of stage 1: "set up scales, tuning and so on right away, look at how any DAW does it".

### Project scale

- In the transport, next to the time signature, a scale button: "C minor" or "Scale: off". Clicking it opens the root and scale picker (the same list as in 2.4). Stored in `PROJECT` as `scaleRoot` (0–11) and `scaleMode` (a key string, `""` = off), one transaction per change.
- New clips (recorded, drawn, captured) get the project scale as their default scale. A clip has a "Own scale" checkbox: if it is off, the clip shows the project scale and changes with it.
- The piano roll highlights the root with a separate row tint, other scale notes normally, notes outside the scale dimmed (as in 2.4).
- **Keyboard in scale**: a toggle in the on-screen keyboard strip (2.6). When on, the Mac keys `A S D F G H J K L ;` play consecutive degrees of the project scale (white rows), the black keys `W E T Y U O P` are not used. An external MIDI keyboard is not affected.
- Scales are described in one place, `core/include/daw/music/Scales.h`: `struct Scale { const char* key; const char* nameRu; std::array<bool,12> pitches; }`, functions `bool inScale (int note, int root, const Scale&)`, `int snapToScale (int note, int root, const Scale&, int direction)` and `int degreeToNote (int degree, int root, const Scale&, int baseOctaveNote)`. Test in `core_tests`.

### Tuning (Tune)

- Not in settings ("Audio" tab), and not in "Create ▸ Project…"; tuning lives in the project: `PROJECT.tuningA4` (Hz, 415–466, default 440). No field in the transport is needed: it is in the "Project Properties" window (`Cmd+Alt+P`, a new small window: default tempo, time signature, scale, tuning, author, notes).
- The built-in Prisma receives the tuning through the global parameter `tuneA4` (not automatable, not saved in presets); the engine sets it on all instances when a project loads and when it changes. Note frequency: `tuningA4 * 2^((note - 69) / 12)`. Tuning is not passed to third-party plugins; the field's tooltip says so: "Built-in synths. For third-party plugins, set the tuning in the plugin itself".
- Microtonal tunings (`.scl`/`.tun`, MTS-ESP) are not included; they belong to the synth stages (09/10).

### Transposition

- In the clip properties panel (piano roll), a "Transpose" field with `−12 −1 +1 +12` buttons: shifts the selected notes (or all), one transaction. With the clip scale on, `+1/−1` move by scale degrees (`snapToScale`), `±12` always move by an octave.
- `Cmd+Shift+↑/↓` in the piano roll does the same for the selection (see 99-details, stage 2).
- On the track, a "Transpose" field in the detail panel (−48…+48 semitones, a live parameter that does not change notes): shifts everything going into the track's instrument, including live playing. Stored in `TRACK.transpose`.

## Task 2.8. Clip clicks and view switching

- Already done in PR #4: a click selects a clip, the triangle on the left launches it, ▶ after ■ resumes the Session clips that were playing.
- A double-click on a clip opens it in the piano roll (2.4) and shows the detail panel if it is hidden. A double-click on an empty slot creates a 1-bar clip and opens it right away.

## Tests

1. **Metronome.** Render 2 bars at 120 BPM, 48 kHz: the first non-zero samples of the clicks are at positions `k * 24000` ± 0, the downbeat is louder by 6 ± 0.5 dB. Metronome off: silence.
2. **Count-in.** With a 1-bar count-in, notes played (injected into the event queue) at 1.1.1 after the count-in are recorded at the start of the clip.
3. **Recording into a slot.** Feed 8 note-on/off on beats through a fake MIDI input, stop recording: a 2-bar clip, 8 notes at the right positions (accounting for the latency adjustment), correct lengths.
4. **Hanging notes.** A note-on without a note-off by the end of recording is closed at the clip end.
5. **Overdub.** Two overdub passes produce the union of notes; `undo` removes only the second pass.
6. **Quantize** (core): 100% strength puts notes on the grid, 50% halfway, 50% swing moves the second eighths by 1/32, triplets are counted from the bar.
7. **Capture while playing.** A buffer with a 6-bar phrase played with the transport running → an 8-bar clip, starting at the bar of the first note.
8. **Capture while stopped.** A phrase of 16th notes at 128 BPM with ±10 ms jitter → `estimateTempo` returns 128 ± 0.5; a 4-bar clip.
9. **Phrase repetition.** Two identical 4-bar passes → one 4-bar pass is captured.
10. **Controllers.** Recorded mod wheel plays back with the same values at the same positions (± 1 sample at the same sample rate).
11. **Piano roll (model).** Moving, resizing and copying notes through `ClipRef` keep them sorted; a 50-step gesture is one transaction.
12. **Real time.** With `DAW_RT_CHECKS=ON`, recording 4 minutes of injected events does not allocate memory in the audio thread.
13. **Scales** (core): `inScale`, `snapToScale` up and down, `degreeToNote` for major, minor and pentatonic, including crossing an octave.
14. **Tuning.** Prisma with `tuneA4 = 432`, note 69, sine without detune: fundamental frequency by FFT 432 ± 0.5 Hz.
15. **Transposition.** `+1` in C major moves E to F and F to G; `TRACK.transpose = +12` raises a live note and a clip note by an octave.
